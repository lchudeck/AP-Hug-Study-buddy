How a ninth grader should use this guide

  1. Learn one CED topic at a time. Start with the plain-English explanation and master vocabulary.
  2. Retrieve before rereading. Use flashcards and short questions without looking at notes.
  3. Apply the idea. Explain a map, scenario, model, or data pattern.
  4. Review mistakes. Study Buddy returns to weak topics and common misconceptions.
  5. Prove mastery over time. One correct answer is not treated as permanent mastery.

The seven AP Human Geography units

Unit 1: Thinking Geographically

Maps, data, spatial concepts, scale, human-environment interaction, and regions.

Unit 2: Population and Migration

Population patterns, demographic change, migration, policies, and population composition.

Unit 3: Cultural Patterns

Culture, language, religion, identity, landscapes, diffusion, and globalization.

Unit 4: Political Patterns

States, nations, boundaries, territoriality, governance, devolution, and representation.

Unit 5: Agriculture

Agricultural origins, land use, production systems, food networks, and rural change.

Unit 6: Cities

Urbanization, city models, land use, infrastructure, sustainability, and inequality.

Unit 7: Development and Industry

Development measures, economic sectors, industrial location, trade, and globalization.

Skills that earn points

Read spatial evidence

Identify patterns in maps, graphs, population pyramids, tables, images, and short source sets.

Use geographic concepts

Move beyond memorized definitions by connecting a concept to the exact scenario.

Explain relationships

State a cause, mechanism, or consequence clearly. A particular connector word is not required.

Evaluate and compare

Use accurate criteria, recognize tradeoffs, and support the conclusion with geographic evidence.
