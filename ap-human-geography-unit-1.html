High-priority distinctions

  • Reference vs. thematic maps: where features are versus the pattern of a selected variable.
  • GIS vs. GPS vs. remote sensing: analyze layers, determine location, or collect imagery/data from a distance.
  • Site vs. situation: characteristics of the place itself versus its relative location and connections.
  • Map scale vs. scale of analysis: map distance relationship versus the level at which data are examined.
  • Formal, functional, and perceptual regions: shared trait, node/flow, or people's ideas.

Apply it

Do not stop at naming a choropleth map or defining scale. Explain what spatial pattern the evidence shows, why the pattern may occur, and how projection, classification, aggregation, or scale could affect the conclusion.
