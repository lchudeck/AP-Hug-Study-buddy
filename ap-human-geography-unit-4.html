Core ideas

High-priority distinctions

  • State vs. nation: sovereign political territory versus people with shared identity.
  • Nation-state, multinational state, and stateless nation: relationships between political boundaries and cultural identity.
  • Federal vs. unitary systems: constitutionally divided power versus centralized authority.
  • Packing vs. cracking: concentrating or dividing voters to affect representation.
  • Devolution vs. secession: transfer of authority downward versus formal withdrawal.

Apply it

Political geography questions often ask how boundaries, identity, resources, representation, or scale can create cooperation or conflict. Explain the mechanism, not merely the vocabulary term.
