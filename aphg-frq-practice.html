What strong APHG FRQ responses do

Task verbWhat the response needsCommon mistake
IdentifyName the correct concept, pattern, process, or example.Writing a long paragraph instead of giving the requested answer.
DefineGive the accurate meaning in your own words.Repeating the term without explaining it.
DescribeProvide an accurate characteristic, pattern, or example.Explaining why when the prompt only asks what.
ExplainConnect a cause, mechanism, or process to an effect.Naming two related facts without connecting them.
CompareState a meaningful similarity or difference using the same criterion.Describing each item separately without comparison.
EvaluateMake a supported judgment that recognizes relevant effects or tradeoffs.Giving an opinion without geographic evidence.
Important: Explain responses need clear causal reasoning, but students do not have to use a specific word such as “because” or “therefore” to earn a point.

Point-by-point coaching

Each lettered part is treated as its own task so students can see exactly what to revise.

Source-based practice

Students practice using maps, charts, short passages, and paired sources rather than memorization alone.

Private feedback

The built-in checker runs in the browser. It is coaching—not official College Board scoring.

A simple revision routine

  1. Label every part.
  2. Underline the task verb mentally.
  3. Answer only what that part asks.
  4. Add a specific APHG concept, piece of evidence, mechanism, or consequence.
  5. Check whether the sentence actually connects to the scenario.
