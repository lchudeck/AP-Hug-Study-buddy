# AP Study Buddy: 28-minute classroom pilot

**Status: prepared, not run.** Use after the release blockers in PILOT_RELEASE_DECISION.md are cleared. Until then, an optional supervised usability observation must use teacher judgment for every FRQ point and cannot validate trustworthy automatic scoring.

## Teacher setup

Choose 6–9 volunteers, ideally 2–3 per unit, including learners who need vocabulary or reading support. Each student completes **one** unit in one session. Do not ask a struggling student to finish three unit journeys in 28 minutes. Use ordinary accommodations, including read-aloud, extra time, and assistive technology; record accommodation type without identifying the learner. Adjust timing as needed rather than removing access supports.

Use paper booklets labeled U1-A, U1-B, etc., with no names or identifier lookup key. Keep papers locally under normal classroom practice. Do not upload student answers, names, screenshots, or other identifying information to external feedback forms or development services. Use synthetic examples for any later bug report unless a separate privacy plan is approved. Do not click the site's feedback/report submission controls for this pilot.

Prepare baseline and exit pages separately. Half of each unit group uses Form A first; half uses Form B first. Do not show answer keys, baseline corrections, or the other form during practice. Keep the same scoring criteria and accommodations for both forms. With 2–3 students per unit, counterbalancing is approximate.

## Schedule and student directions

| Time | Activity | Directions |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 min | Five-item baseline | Answer your assigned form on paper without the site. A brief phrase can be enough. Guess if unsure. |
| 4–7 min | Learn and find vocabulary | Open https://www.apstudybuddy.com. Choose your unit. Use “I don’t understand this yet” or Key Terms. Read one short explanation and a concrete example for your assigned focus below. |
| 7–14 min | Apply and recover | Open Practice Questions or Unit Review in the same unit. Answer, read feedback after a mistake, then try a different question about that concept. Do not deliberately miss if you already understand it. If the retry repeats the same question or changes concepts, mark that on your sheet. |
| 14–22 min | FRQ and revision | Open FRQ Coach and your unit prompt. Answer only the two assigned parts below, labeled with their letters. Save the first response on paper before submitting. Read the point feedback. Revise once in your own words, save it on paper, and submit the revision before opening models. Blanks in other parts are expected; the /7 total is not your grade. |
| 22–26 min | Five-item exit check | Close or cover the site. Complete the parallel form independently. |
| 26–28 min | Student feedback | Complete the short instrument below. |

If a student cannot finish in the window, record the stopping point and elapsed time. Never supply answers to improve completion figures. If a new same-concept question does not appear within the session, record “transfer opportunity not provided”; do not count a repeated item as transfer. The protocol's actual feasibility must be established with students.

| Unit | Assigned focus | Coach parts and teacher criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Site/situation, regional versus local analysis, using GIS evidence | **B:** define situation as relative location/connections. **E:** explain how regional aggregation can hide differences that local neighborhood analysis reveals. |
| 2 | Aging, dependency, workforce effects, DTM | **B:** dependent ages compared with working-age ages in that order. **D:** arriving working-age migrants add labor supply or fill vacancies; an answer claiming a smaller workforce contradicts that mechanism. |
| 3 | Relocation/stimulus diffusion, shared languages, local cultural change | **B:** people move and carry cultural traits. **E:** a communication/trade/travel mechanism accelerates or extends cultural spread, with the causal direction stated correctly. |

## Unit 1 forms: five points each

### Form A

1. A town grew beside fertile soil and a navigable river. Name the concept describing those physical characteristics.
2. A warehouse locates near a port and two highways to reach buyers. Name the location concept illustrated.
3. A regional map shows an average 12-minute clinic trip. A neighborhood map shows 8 minutes in North and 35 in South. What useful pattern does the regional average hide?
4. You have clinic locations and household income in a GIS. Describe one comparison that could identify unequal access.
5. North has 8 clinics for 8,000 residents; South has 2 for 8,000. Which neighborhood appears less served, using the available evidence?

### Form B

1. A settlement began on a sheltered harbor with fresh water. Name the concept describing those physical characteristics.
2. A factory chooses a rail junction that connects it to suppliers and customers. Name the location concept illustrated.
3. A regional map shows an average 15-minute food-store trip. A neighborhood map shows 10 minutes in East and 40 in West. What useful pattern does the regional average hide?
4. You have grocery-store locations and car-ownership rates in a GIS. Describe one comparison that could identify unequal access.
5. East has 3 clinics for 9,000 residents; West has 9 for 9,000. Which neighborhood appears less served, using the available evidence?

## Unit 2 forms: five points each

### Form A

1. Birth and death rates are both low, with near-zero natural increase. Which DTM stage best fits the four-stage model?
2. A population has 20 people ages 0–14, 60 ages 15–64, and 20 ages 65+. Write the dependency fraction before multiplying by 100.
3. A country's pyramid has a narrower base and a larger older-age share than 20 years ago. Identify one likely economic pressure.
4. Working-age immigrants arrive where jobs are unfilled. Explain how they could address a labor shortage.
5. A government funds childcare to encourage births. Why will resulting births not fill current job vacancies immediately?

### Form B

1. Birth and death rates are both low, with slow natural increase. Which DTM stage best fits the four-stage model?
2. A population has 30 people ages 0–14, 80 ages 15–64, and 10 ages 65+. Write the dependency fraction before multiplying by 100.
3. A country's pyramid has fewer children and a growing share over age 65 compared with its earlier pyramid. Identify one likely economic pressure.
4. Working-age newcomers settle in a region with vacant healthcare jobs. Explain how they could address a labor shortage.
5. A government provides birth grants. Why will resulting births not solve this year's shortage of workers?

## Unit 3 forms: five points each

### Form A

1. Migrants bring a religious festival to their new country. Name the diffusion type.
2. A global restaurant chain changes its recipes to fit local religious food practices. Name the diffusion type.
3. A street has an old place of worship, a later rail depot, and recent multilingual shops. What concept describes these accumulated historical layers?
4. In a fictional region, 65% in West and 15% in East speak a particular language at home. Which area would you investigate first for visible signs in that language? Explain using the data; do not claim everyone speaks it.
5. Explain how a common second language can help traders with different first languages interact.

### Form B

1. Families move overseas and introduce their traditional food festival there. Name the diffusion type.
2. An international sport spreads to a new region where its equipment and rules are adapted to local traditions. Name the diffusion type.
3. A neighborhood retains an old temple, a later factory, and new immigrant storefronts. What concept describes these accumulated historical layers?
4. In a fictional region, 20% in North and 70% in South use a particular language at home. Which area would you investigate first for visible signs in that language? Explain using the data; do not claim everyone speaks it.
5. Explain how a shared bridge language can help travelers with different first languages exchange information.

## Teacher key and scoring

Score each baseline/exit item **0 or 1**, content only. Accept clear paraphrases and plausible spelling mistakes. Do not infer a missing causal link. Do not grade grammar. These are original low-stakes pilot checks, not official College Board items or a comprehensive unit assessment.

| Item | Unit 1 A / B | Unit 2 A / B | Unit 3 A / B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Site / site | Stage 4 / Stage 4 (the prompt specifies four-stage) | Relocation / relocation |
| 2 | Situation / situation | (20+20)/60 / (30+10)/80; equivalent 40/60 / 40/80 accepted | Stimulus / stimulus |
| 3 | Unequal clinic access, especially South's longer trip / unequal food access, especially West's longer trip | Rising pension or healthcare costs, or labor shortage; specific effect required / same | Sequent occupance / sequent occupance |
| 4 | Compare clinic proximity/access across income groups / compare food access with low car ownership | Arrivals increase available workers or fill vacant jobs / same; connect newcomers to available labor | West: 65% exceeds 15% / South: 70% exceeds 20% |
| 5 | South: fewer clinics for equal population / East: fewer clinics for equal population | Babies need years to reach working age / same | Shared language enables communication/trade/information exchange across first-language groups / same |

For Unit 1 item 5, this is an inference from a limited clinic-per-population comparison, not proof of travel access, clinic quality or capacity. For Unit 3 item 4, home-language shares motivate fieldwork but do not guarantee signage or establish its cause.

## FRQ teacher review: two parts, first and revised

Use the unit criteria above: 0 or 1 per part, total /2 for each attempt. Mark the student response **before** comparing with the app result. Review every claimed verified point. Log whether feedback accurately describes the missing reasoning and whether the revision fixes it independently. A higher app total can reflect phrase matching or answer copying; it is not evidence of learning.

Record these columns on paper: anonymous code; unit; form order; baseline /5; exit /5; first FRQ /2; revised FRQ /2; app false-verified points; valid responses flagged for review; technical blocker; workflow completed; teacher rescue count/type; accommodations; same-item versus new-item retry; corrected misconception; elapsed minutes.

“Teacher rescue” means a navigation, interpretation or writing prompt needed beyond the printed directions. Ordinary accommodations and scheduled teacher scoring do not count as rescue. Report accommodation use separately rather than excluding those students from the denominator. Do not treat an unfinished student as missing data without reporting them.

## Student feedback (paper)

Circle **1 strongly disagree, 2 disagree, 3 agree, 4 strongly agree**:

- I knew what to click and do next.
- Definitions and examples were clear.
- Feedback helped me understand why my answer needed work.
- I could continue without teacher help, using my usual accommodations.

Then answer:

- Where did you feel confused or stuck? “Nowhere” is okay.
- One idea I changed my mind about was ____. I now think ____ because ____.
- What part helped most? What should change first?

## Proposed success thresholds and decision

- Zero critical technical blockers or silent loss of work.
- Zero false verified FRQ points in teacher-reviewed samples.
- At least 80% complete the planned workflow without teacher rescue beyond ordinary accommodations. Report the numerator and denominator; with 6 students this requires 5/6, with 9 it requires 8/9.
- Median paired exit-minus-baseline score exceeds zero. Also report median baseline, median exit, range, form order and each unit's results. A tiny pilot is descriptive, not a causal study.
- Each participant can identify at least one corrected misconception; report the proportion meeting this goal and distinguish accurate explanations from vague claims.

No pilot results exist yet. Any false verified point fails the scoring threshold and returns that criterion to review. If usability passes but learning does not improve, adjust only the observed learning failure. Do not expand to an unsupported independent or graded assignment based solely on completion or satisfaction.
