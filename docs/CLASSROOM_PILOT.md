# Guided FRQ writing: printable classroom pilot

Prepared, not run. One unit per student; 20–30 minutes. Use the approved candidate only after release authorization. Production currently retains the old scored Coach.

## Teacher directions
Use 6–9 volunteers across Units 1–3, including students needing reading/vocabulary support. Keep ordinary accommodations consistent across baseline/exit. Give anonymous paper booklets with a unit and random letter; keep no name lookup. Do not upload real writing, screenshots or identifying data to feedback services. Record technical failures with synthetic reproductions. Students may copy their original, revision and transfer onto paper for teacher review. App work stays in the current browser-tab session; closure normally clears it, though browsers may restore it. Copy to paper before closing.

| Minutes | Student directions |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Complete your assigned baseline form independently on paper, without examples or the site. |
| 4–17 | Open FRQ Coach, select your unit, read task guidance and annotated examples. Write the new response, use the review questions, revise and explain your change. Compare the example after revising. Use “Show an example for help” if stuck; mark that support on paper. |
| 17–21 | Complete the different in-app transfer scenario in your own words. Copy original, revision, reflection and transfer to paper for teacher review. |
| 21–26 | Cover the site and complete the other form independently. |
| 26–28 | Identify one misconception corrected or a question remaining. Record confusing directions, lost work or needed help. |

Timing is provisional; allow accommodations/extra time and record unfinished steps. Do not coach answers to improve completion statistics. Half each unit group uses A as baseline and B as exit, half reverses. Keep keys and other form hidden. Existing pilot's site/situation, aging/immigration and diffusion focuses are reused; new parallel forms match the short lessons.

## Independent baseline and parallel exit forms
For each form, answer both parts; brief sufficient answers are welcome.

| Unit | Form A | Form B |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define situation. A district reports a 20-minute library trip; North is 5 and South 35. Explain what local analysis reveals that the district average hides. | Define situation. A county reports a 30-minute hospital trip; East is 10 and West 50. Explain what local analysis reveals that the county average hides. |
| 2 | Describe one economic pressure from population aging. Factories have retiring machinists and vacancies. Qualified working-age machinists immigrate and may work. Explain how they can address the shortage. | Describe one economic pressure from population aging. Farms have retiring equipment operators and vacancies. Qualified working-age operators immigrate and may work. Explain how they can address the shortage. |
| 3 | Describe stimulus diffusion using an example. A craft tutorial posted online is watched and shared abroad. Explain how technology spreads this practice. | Describe stimulus diffusion using a different example. A cooking demonstration streamed online is watched and shared abroad. Explain how technology spreads this practice. |

## Teacher criteria (paper only; never app-generated)
Review content, not spelling/grammar. Use the same four criteria for each parallel pair: each demonstrated criterion = 1, absent/incorrect = 0. This is a local pilot measure, not an AP score. Accept valid alternative wording and short sufficient answers.

| Unit | Four baseline/exit criteria |
|---|---|
| 1 | Situation means relative location/connections; distinguishes local differences; uses relevant provided travel evidence; explains aggregation hides variation rather than claiming the local map causes inequality. |
| 2 | Gives a specific economic pressure (e.g., unfilled jobs, pension or health-service costs); identifies qualified arriving workers; explains filling vacancies left by retirees; connects this to reduced shortage without reversing direction or guaranteeing all problems end. |
| 3 | Spreading underlying idea; adaptation in new setting in the stimulus example; online access lets distant viewers encounter/learn the practice; viewing/sharing extends cultural spread without requiring migration. |

For the original and revision, record: answers actual task; accurate relevant idea; evidence/scenario connection; mechanism and causal direction when required. Note what changed, whether it became more accurate, and whether the transfer independently applies reasoning in a new context. Mark example support separately. Check whether examples and directions were accurate, accessible and useful. Teacher judgments about student writing are separate from app completion.

## Anonymous observation record
Unit / form order / accommodations / completed step / elapsed time / teacher rescue beyond accommodations / example help / critical blocker or lost work / baseline 0–4 / exit 0–4 / original reasoning notes / revision changes / transfer accuracy / misconception or remaining question.

## Proposed success targets — unmeasured
- Zero critical technical blockers or silent loss of work.
- Zero misleading automatic grades or correctness claims.
- At least 80% finish without teacher rescue beyond ordinary accommodations.
- Median independent exit performance exceeds median baseline performance; inspect individual results and unit differences too.
- Teacher review finds stronger geographic reasoning in revisions and examines transfer.
- Students identify at least one misconception corrected. Record remaining questions honestly when none was corrected.

A simulated revision is not evidence of learning. Small pilot results do not establish broad efficacy. Use findings to decide whether one bounded correction is needed before expanding.
